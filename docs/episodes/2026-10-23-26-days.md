## Reaction time and a faster detector
Where the ~8 minutes from onset to recovery go today: 2 min to detect (3 slow samples in a row), ~40 s `diag`, ~3.5 min episode tests,
then the cure; speed comes back within ~1-2 min of the cure. The episode tests have now run in 22 episodes (the plan was to stop after
10-15); switching them off (`EPTEST=off`) alone would bring the cure ~3.5 min earlier.

Detection itself cannot simply use fewer samples. Replayed on all HTTP-200 samples 2026-10-19..26 (9 832): a single sample >= 3 s happened
17 times outside episodes (>= 2 s: 34), and two slow samples in a row 3 times; each would have been a cure in the good state.

Packet reordering separates the two states cleanly. In the pcaps on TrueNAS every developed episode shows 2-16 out-of-order packets among
the first 100 segments of nearly every download, the references never show any. Since 2026-10-26 15:47 the light probe reads this from its own
socket (`tcpi_rcv_ooopack`, metric `u5g_probe_light_ooo_packets`, same 1 MB download, no extra traffic), and the watchdog logs every slow
sample that has out-of-order packets as `SHADOW:`. With `LIGHT_OOO` > 0 one such sample would start the episode, about 2 minutes earlier.
Not yet known: whether the false slow samples (load, server hiccups) are free of reordering, and whether the very first slow sample of an
episode already shows it. The shadow lines of the next episodes answer both; the rule stays off until then and until the owner approves.

Estimated onset -> cure with both changes: ~1-1.5 min (detect on the first slow sample, ~40 s `diag`), against ~6 min now.
